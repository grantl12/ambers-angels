# Amber's Angels: Volunteer Training Guide
## Version 1.0 — Carroll County Pilot Program

---

> **Welcome.** By joining Amber's Angels, you are choosing to use your time, your vehicle, and your local knowledge to help bring missing children and vulnerable adults home safely. This guide covers everything you need to know before your first active mission.

---

## Part 1: Who We Are & What You're Joining

### The Mission

Amber's Angels is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit that deploys a network of volunteer search nodes — people just like you, in their own cars or with their own drones — during active AMBER Alerts and other government-issued missing person emergencies.

When an alert fires, our platform activates. You receive a push notification. You drive your normal routes (or assigned sector) with our app running. Our AI scans your camera feed in real time. If it spots a match, a trained coordinator reviews it and contacts law enforcement with a GPS-precise lead.

**You don't need to be a cop. You don't need special gear.** You need a smartphone, a car mount, and the willingness to help.

### The Three Things You Must Understand

1. **You are a sensor, not an investigator.** Your job is to drive and let the AI work. You do not confront suspects. You do not follow vehicles. You do not contact law enforcement directly.
2. **Privacy is non-negotiable.** The app does not record or store video. You must not record independently. You must not share any information about active searches on social media.
3. **The human coordinator is the decision-maker.** No AI result ever goes to law enforcement without a human reviewing it first. That human is not you — it is a trained coordinator. Trust the chain of command.

## Part 2: Volunteer Roles

### Role A: Ground Vehicle Volunteer (Mobile Sensor Node)

**What you need:**
- An approved smartphone (iOS 14+ or Android 10+)
- A windshield or dashboard car mount with a clear forward view
- A valid driver's license and a reliable vehicle
- Completion of this training guide + signed Data Use Agreement
- Passed background check

**What you do during an alert:**
- Accept the mission in the app
- Mount your phone and drive your assigned sector (or your normal route if no sector is assigned)
- Keep the app running and the camera unobstructed
- Do not speed, break traffic laws, or take any action that endangers yourself or others

**What you never do:**
- Physically follow a suspect vehicle
- Attempt to stop or confront anyone
- Share details of the search publicly
- Screenshot or save anything from the app

---

### Role B: Drone Pilot (Aerial Sensor Node)

**Additional requirements beyond Role A:**
- **FAA Part 107 Remote Pilot Certificate** (required, no exceptions)
- Must carry proof of certification on all missions
- Must follow all FAA airspace rules for the operational area
- Must maintain **Visual Line of Sight (VLOS)** at all times
- No nighttime operations without an FAA waiver
- Must carry current UAS liability insurance

**What drone pilots add:**
Aerial nodes can cover terrain that ground vehicles cannot — fields, wooded areas, parking lots, highway interchange areas. Your camera altitude and angle of view give coordinators a different perspective that often catches what ground nodes miss.

**Pre-flight checklist (every mission):**
- [ ] Check NOTAM for local airspace restrictions
- [ ] Confirm weather is within safe operating limits (< 25 mph winds)
- [ ] Verify drone battery is fully charged
- [ ] Check that camera lens is clean
- [ ] Confirm you have line-of-sight to your planned flight zone
- [ ] Log into the Amber's Angels app and confirm mission assignment

---

### Role C: Mission Coordinator (Advanced — Separate Training Required)

Coordinators review AI-flagged detections and decide whether to escalate to law enforcement. This role requires a separate application, additional vetting, and dedicated training. If you are interested, contact admin@ambersangels.org after completing your first 3 active missions as a ground volunteer.

## Part 3: The App — Step by Step

### Installation

1. Download the **Amber's Angels** app from the App Store (iOS) or Google Play (Android)
2. Create an account using the email address you registered with
3. Complete identity verification in-app (you will be prompted to upload your ID)
4. Wait for account approval — you will receive an email within 48–72 hours
5. Once approved, complete the in-app training module before your first mission

### Alert Notifications

When an AMBER Alert or other supported alert fires in your area, you will receive a push notification with:
- Alert type (AMBER / Silver / Mattie's Call / Purple / Blue)
- Brief vehicle description (make, model, color, plate if known)
- Your assigned sector (if one is available) or a general coverage request

**You always have the choice to accept or decline.** Declining a mission never affects your standing. We only want volunteers who can deploy safely.

Tap **END MISSION** when:
- You need to stop for any reason
- You've completed your assigned sector
- The alert is cancelled (you will be notified automatically)
- You feel unsafe for any reason — no explanation required

## Part 4: The Privacy Rules (Non-Negotiable)

These are not suggestions. Violating any of these will result in immediate removal from the program.

### What the App Does
- Processes your camera feed **in real-time, in memory only**
- Deletes each frame immediately after processing
- Sends only high-confidence detection events (not frames or video) to the coordination hub
- Tracks your GPS location during active missions for sector assignment and coverage mapping

### What the App Does NOT Do
- Record or store any video footage
- Run when you are not in an active mission
- Access your contacts, messages, photos, or any data outside the app's scope

### What YOU Must Not Do

| ❌ Never | ✅ Instead |
| :--- | :--- |
| Post about active searches on social media | Wait until the official resolution is announced |
| Share screenshots of the app during a mission | Screenshots are disabled during active missions |
| Tell people which roads you're covering | Operational details are confidential |
| Record video independently on another device | The app handles all detection — no personal recording |
| Identify yourself publicly as an active volunteer | Your participation is not public information |

**Why this matters:** Social media posts about active searches can tip off suspects, compromise ongoing operations, and undermine the trust of the communities and law enforcement agencies we serve. One post can end a search.

## Part 5: Safety Protocols

### Personal Safety — Always First

- **Never follow a suspect vehicle.** If the app flags a potential match near you, continue driving normally. Do not slow down, stop near the vehicle, or make eye contact with occupants.
- **Never confront anyone.** You are a sensor. Detection and response are handled by professionals.
- **Drive safely.** No mission is worth a traffic accident. Follow all traffic laws at all times. If you need to pull over to check the app, do so safely before touching your phone.
- **Trust your instincts.** If any situation feels unsafe, end your mission immediately.

### Emergency Situations

If you witness an active emergency (assault, accident, abduction in progress):
1. **Call 911 immediately** — this is always the first step
2. End your mission in the app
3. Follow 911 operator instructions
4. After the situation is resolved, contact admin@ambersangels.org to report the incident

The app does **not** replace 911. We are a search support tool, not an emergency dispatch system.

### Drone Safety (Pilots Only)

- Never fly over people who have not consented to be below your flight path
- Maintain altitude awareness — know your airspace class
- If you lose visual contact with your drone, land immediately
- Never attempt to recover a drone that has landed on private property without permission
- Report any near-miss incidents to admin@ambersangels.org within 24 hours

## Part 6: Understanding the Alerts

### Alert Types & What They Mean

| Alert | Missing Person | Key Detail |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **AMBER Alert** | Child (under 18) believed abducted | Highest priority; time-critical |
| **Silver Alert** | Senior with dementia or Alzheimer's | May be on foot or in own vehicle |
| **Mattie's Call** | Adult with developmental/intellectual disability | May not respond to their name |
| **Purple Alert** | Person with a disability (varies by state) | Check alert for specific details |
| **Blue Alert** | Endangered law enforcement officer | Suspect is considered armed/dangerous — **extra caution** |

### What's in an Alert Notification

Every mission notification includes:
- **Alert type** and issuing agency
- **Subject description** (if released): name, age, physical description
- **Vehicle of interest** (if known): make, model, color, license plate
- **Last known location** and direction of travel
- **Alert expiration time** (most AMBER Alerts are 24 hours unless extended)

### When an Alert Ends

Alerts end when:
- The missing person is found (safe or otherwise)
- Law enforcement cancels the search
- The alert expires without extension

You will be notified in-app when your active mission is terminated. Please end your mission promptly when notified.

## Part 7: Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What if I see the actual suspect vehicle during a mission?**
A: Do not follow it. Do not stop near it. Continue driving normally. The app will have already flagged it if it's a match. Your coordinator is handling it. Call 911 only if you observe an active emergency.

**Q: Can I volunteer if I don't have a car mount?**
A: Not for active driving missions — a mounted, stable camera is required for accurate detection. Car mounts are inexpensive ($15–$30) and worth the investment. Contact admin@ambersangels.org if cost is a barrier.

**Q: What if an alert fires while I'm at work or otherwise unavailable?**
A: Decline the mission in the app. It takes two taps. We will never penalize you for being unavailable.

**Q: Can I participate in searches outside my county?**
A: Yes, once our multi-county deployment launches. During the Carroll County pilot, please focus on in-county coverage.

**Q: I'm a retired law enforcement officer. Can I serve as a coordinator?**
A: Your background is extremely valuable and we want to talk to you. Please email admin@ambersangels.org with your experience.

**Q: What happens to the detection data after an alert ends?**
A: GPS telemetry is retained for 90 days for mission analysis and deleted. Detection records (plate + VMMC data for confirmed leads) are retained for 1 year for law enforcement reference on the active case. Raw video is never stored.

**Q: Does the app drain my phone battery?**
A: Active AI processing does consume significant battery. We strongly recommend a car charger for all missions. Battery life tips are available in the app's settings panel.

**Q: I saw something during a search that I think might be relevant but the app didn't flag it. What do I do?**
A: End your mission and immediately call the non-emergency line for the county where the alert is active. Do not attempt to report it through the Amber's Angels app — we are not a reporting channel, we are a detection tool.

## Part 8: Code of Conduct

As an Amber's Angels volunteer, you represent our organization, our law enforcement partners, and the families we serve. We expect:

1. **Professionalism** — in the field and in how you discuss your participation
2. **Privacy discipline** — strict adherence to the data rules in Part 4
3. **Safety first** — no mission is more important than your safety or others'
4. **Honesty** — if you make an error, report it; if you witness a protocol violation, report it
5. **Respect** — for law enforcement, for your fellow volunteers, for the communities you drive through

Violations of the Code of Conduct or any training requirement will result in suspension or permanent removal from the program, depending on severity.
