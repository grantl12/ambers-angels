The Coverage Gap — Series Introduction

The Coverage Gap

By Grant Lindberg, Founder & Executive Director — Amber's Angels Inc.  ·  June 2026

As a signals intelligence professional and leader, I was part of routine audits to ensure constitutional compliance. Understanding the legalities of surveillance was in the job description. So when I say the privacy architecture of Amber's Angels isn't a marketing decision — I mean it in a very specific way.

Two Tools. Different Questions.

Fixed license plate readers — systems like Flock Safety — are genuinely useful. They've solved crimes. They've located missing people. They've given law enforcement a retrospective intelligence capability that didn't exist a decade ago.

I'm not here to tear them down.

But they answer a specific question: what happened, and who was there? They're retrospective tools. They shine when an investigator needs to know what vehicle passed a particular intersection last Tuesday at 2pm.

Amber's Angels answers a different question: what's happening right now, and where is that vehicle in the next sixty seconds? We're a real-time activation tool. We shine when a child is missing and every second counts.

Neither system answers the other's question. A jurisdiction with only fixed ALPR has retrospective intelligence and no real-time activation. A jurisdiction with only Amber's Angels has real-time activation and no investigative reach-back. A jurisdiction with both has the full picture.

That's the frame for everything that follows in this series.

"Fixed ALPR asks: what happened? Amber's Angels asks: what's happening right now? These are different questions. They deserve different tools."

What This Series Is

Over the next several weeks I'm going to work through the specific gaps — geographic, architectural, and operational — that motivated every design decision in Amber's Angels. Some of these posts will be uncomfortable. I'll acknowledge what always-on systems do better than we do. I'll be honest about what we can't do yet. I'll describe capabilities we're building, not just capabilities we have.

What I won't do is oversell. The law enforcement and public safety professionals who might use this platform deserve accurate information, not marketing copy. The communities whose children we're trying to protect deserve the same.

Who This Is For

If you work in public safety, law enforcement, emergency management, or child protection — this series is written with you in mind. I'd genuinely welcome your perspective in the comments. The people closest to these problems see things I don't from a basement in Carrollton, Georgia.

If you're a technologist, civil liberties advocate, or grant-making organization interested in responsible AI for public safety — I think you'll find something useful here too.

If you're a parent — welcome. This one's for you most of all.
