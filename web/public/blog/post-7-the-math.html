The Context That Makes These Numbers Matter

According to OJJDP research, 76% of child abduction victims who are murdered are killed within the first three hours of the abduction. Three hours is the window. Everything about missing persons response technology should be evaluated against that window.

The AMBER Alert that activates our platform doesn't fire the moment an incident occurs. It fires after law enforcement responds, assesses the situation, coordinates with state emergency management, and completes the alert issuance process. That takes time — on average, somewhere between 30 minutes and 3 hours.

So by the time our platform activates, the three-hour window is already partially elapsed. The question isn't "how fast are we from incident to notification?" It's "how fast are we from alert activation to actionable intelligence in a coordinator's hands?"

The answer, in our June 7th test: under a minute for the phone pipeline. Under ten seconds for the drone pipeline.

What "Coordinator Notification" Actually Means

When we say "coordinator notification," we mean a Discord alert with the following information, delivered to the coordinator channel:

Plate text and ALPR confidence score. Vehicle color and body type from YOLO classification. Vehicle profile match verdict — does the detected vehicle match the alert description? GPS coordinates with a map link. Timestamp. Frame image attached.

Not a raw detection event. A human-reviewable lead package. Everything a coordinator needs to make a dispatch decision in one message.

Under a minute from alert activation to that lead package in a coordinator's hands. That's the number.

What These Numbers Don't Mean

They don't mean every activation will produce a detection in under a minute. They don't mean every detection will be at 99% confidence. They don't mean the platform will find every suspect vehicle in every search.

They mean that when the conditions are right — when a volunteer is in coverage, when the vehicle passes within camera range, when the plate is readable — the pipeline from detection to coordinator notification is fast. Very fast.

Building the volunteer density to make those conditions reliable is the work of the next year. That's what the Carroll County pilot is for.
