The Coverage Gap — Post 2 of 8

A content series on the future of public safety AI

Different Questions

By Grant Lindberg, Founder & Executive Director — Amber's Angels Inc.  ·  July 1, 2026

Flock Safety and Amber's Angels are better together than either is alone. That's not a marketing line — it's the honest architecture of the problem. Here's exactly what I mean.

What Fixed ALPR Does Well

Flock Safety reads plates in real time at every camera it covers. It also reaches back — when a detective needs to know what vehicle passed a specific intersection at a specific time, Flock answers that question with precision. That dual capability — real-time and retrospective — has closed cases, found missing people, and given law enforcement intelligence that didn't exist a decade ago.

I want to be direct about this because I think some of the criticism of always-on ALPR conflates the tool with the deployment. The tool is genuinely useful. The question is which problems it's the right tool for.

"What Flock cannot do is move. A camera covers the intersection it sits on. Everything else is a gap."

The Geography of the Problem

In a vehicle abduction, the suspect's first goal is to get off the road network that law enforcement is watching. That doesn't require sophistication. It requires knowing what anyone who drives those roads knows: where the cameras are and where they aren't.

Residential streets. Rural routes. Parking lots. Secondary roads between major intersections. These aren't obscure — they're where most driving happens. And they're where fixed ALPR coverage ends.

When a suspect vehicle turns off a covered intersection, the feed goes dark. In real time and in reach-back, that vehicle is invisible to fixed infrastructure until it passes another camera. The gap between cameras is the gap we're trying to close.

Issue

Fixed ALPR provides real-time and retrospective intelligence at fixed camera locations. The roads between those locations — residential streets, rural routes, secondary roads — have no automated coverage during an active missing persons search.

Discussion

In Carroll County, Georgia — our pilot jurisdiction — the road network extends across 503 square miles. A relatively small number of fixed cameras cover major intersections and arterials. The secondary road network, where a suspect is most likely to route to avoid detection, has no fixed coverage. This isn't unique to Carroll County. It describes most of America.

Recommendation

Mobile volunteer coverage deployed during active alerts fills the gap between fixed cameras without replacing fixed infrastructure. The two systems answer different questions and cover different geography.

How Amber's Angels addresses this

Volunteer phones and drones process every vehicle they encounter during an active alert. No fixed installation required. Coverage follows the search, not the infrastructure.

The Honest Comparison

Fixed ALPR

  • Real-time at fixed camera locations
  • Retrospective for all logged vehicles
  • High precision at covered intersections
  • Cannot cover roads without cameras
  • Coverage is static — suspect can route around it
  • Answers: what passed this point?

Amber's Angels

  • Real-time wherever a volunteer drives or flies
  • No retrospective capability — by design
  • Coverage follows the active search
  • Covers residential, rural, secondary roads
  • Coverage is mobile — moves with the suspect
  • Answers: what's in front of a volunteer right now?

Why Both

A jurisdiction with only fixed ALPR has a strong investigative tool and a geographic blind spot that suspects can exploit. A jurisdiction with only Amber's Angels has real-time mobile coverage and no retrospective capability for investigations.

A jurisdiction with both has fixed infrastructure covering the intersections and mobile volunteers covering everything between them. No blind spots in the present moment. Full investigative record for everything that passed a fixed camera.

These tools aren't competing for the same budget line. They're answering different questions across different coverage areas. The combination is stronger than either alone.
