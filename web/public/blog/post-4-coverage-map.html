The Coverage Map Problem

By Grant Lindberg, Founder & Executive Director — Amber's Angels Inc.  ·  July 15, 2026

When I open the Amber's Angels Mission Map and pull up the Coverage Planner, I see the same pattern across every jurisdiction we've modeled. It tells me something important — not just about what's missing, but about what's working.

What the Coverage Planner Shows

Departments deploying Flock Falcons are doing the right thing. They're putting fixed cameras at choke points, major intersections, and high-traffic arterials — the places that give you the most coverage per dollar. Those decisions are defensible. We don't second-guess them.

What the Coverage Planner shows coordinators is both sides of that picture at once: the roads those cameras cover, and the roads they don't. The gaps aren't random. They're predictable — residential streets, rural routes, the secondary roads between intersections. In most jurisdictions, these uncovered roads make up the majority of road mileage. They're also the routes a suspect is most likely to take the moment they realize they're being looked for.

That coverage data is available in real time to coordinators on the Mission Map. When a gap lights up, they can dispatch drones and ground volunteers to cover it with a click. The platform connects the intelligence — here's what's missing — to the action: move someone there. AI intelligence in the service of the people doing the work. That's the point.

"The communities in the biggest gaps are often the ones with the least infrastructure budget. That's not a coincidence — it's the same problem from two angles."

Rural Departments

A small department covering a large rural county faces a coverage problem that money alone can't solve. You can't put a camera on every road in a 500 square mile jurisdiction. The math doesn't work even with a large budget.

A mobile volunteer network changes the math. Volunteers are distributed across the coverage area. When an alert fires, they deploy from wherever they already are — not from a fixed installation, but from their driveways, their neighborhoods, their normal routes. The coverage scales with volunteer density, not infrastructure investment.

For a rural department, this means the possibility of meaningful alert response coverage without a capital investment in fixed infrastructure. The platform costs nothing. The volunteers bring their own hardware.

Tribal Nations

I want to address this specifically because it deserves direct attention.

The Missing and Murdered Indigenous Persons crisis is a national emergency. Indigenous people, particularly women and girls, go missing and are murdered at rates that far exceed national averages — and the cases are systematically underprioritized and underreported. The technology conversation around missing persons response has not adequately addressed tribal jurisdictions.

Tribal nations face every factor that makes coverage gaps worse simultaneously:

Geography: Many tribal land holdings are vast and rural, with limited road infrastructure and no fixed ALPR coverage.

Budget: Tribal law enforcement agencies are often significantly underfunded relative to the jurisdictions they cover.

Jurisdictional complexity: Overlapping federal, state, and tribal jurisdiction creates coordination challenges that delay response.

Community connectivity: Despite infrastructure limitations, tribal communities often have strong internal communication networks and willing community members — exactly the volunteer base a platform like ours needs.

The privacy dimension for tribal jurisdictions

Persistent surveillance data creates specific concerns around tribal sovereignty. An always-on system that retains vehicle movement data raises questions about who controls that data, who can access it, and under what circumstances it can be shared with federal or state agencies. These aren't hypothetical concerns — they reflect real historical experiences with data sovereignty.

Amber's Angels' no-retention architecture is specifically relevant here. No persistent data means no data sovereignty questions. The system activates on a government-issued alert, processes frames in real time, and retains nothing. There is no database to share, subpoena, or misuse.

The Coverage Gap Is a Solvable Problem

Fixed infrastructure will never cover every road. It's not designed to. The answer isn't more cameras — it's a different kind of coverage for the roads between the cameras.

A mobile volunteer network that activates on government-issued alerts, costs nothing to law enforcement, and leaves no trace of the innocent vehicles it scans is a different answer to the coverage problem. Not a replacement for fixed infrastructure. A complement to it, with specific value in exactly the jurisdictions where fixed infrastructure is most limited.

We built this to cover the gaps. The communities in the biggest gaps are the ones we most need to reach.
