When Plates Disappear

By Grant Lindberg, Founder & Executive Director — Amber's Angels Inc.  ·  July 8, 2026

The plate is the primary key. I want to be honest about that before I say anything else — and honest about exactly what our system does when it fails.

How Our Pipeline Actually Works

Every detection in Amber's Angels runs two independent checks simultaneously: a plate read (OpenALPR + Plate Recognizer) and a vehicle profile classification (YOLO). These two signals are compared. How the system responds depends on which signals are present, what they say, and whether they agree.

Plates fail in two distinct ways. The distinction matters for how you think about what we can and can't do.

"Two signals. Plate identity and vehicle profile. When they conflict, or when one is missing entirely, we surface that explicitly rather than hiding it behind a single confidence number."

Failure Mode 1: The Switched Plate

A suspect swaps plates to avoid detection — putting a different vehicle's plates on the suspect car. Our pipeline reads the switched plate, matches it to the watchlist, and then our YOLO vehicle classifier independently examines the actual car.

If the alert says white Ford Bronco but YOLO sees a red VW sedan, the system flags the discrepancy. The confidence score drops by up to 20 points. When the penalized score falls below the mismatch threshold, the notification routes to a dedicated coordinator channel — visually distinct from a standard hit — with the title ⚠️ PLATE MATCH — PROFILE MISMATCH and the vehicle discrepancy spelled out explicitly.

The plate matched. The car didn't. That discrepancy is itself a lead — it tells a coordinator that something doesn't add up and the vehicle is worth a second look. It's not a false positive to be dismissed. It's a signal to investigate.

Current behavior — switched plate

Alert fires to a dedicated mismatch channel with penalized confidence score and explicit vehicle discrepancy noted. Coordinator sees plate match AND vehicle mismatch in a single notification. The channel separation means a coordinator can subscribe to mismatch alerts with appropriate skepticism — they're investigative leads, not confirmed identifications.

Failure Mode 2: No Readable Plate

The plate is covered, removed, or so damaged it can't be read. In this case, until recently, our pipeline exited immediately. The vehicle was invisible to our system even if YOLO perfectly identified it as matching the alert profile.

That gap is now closed.

When no readable plate is detected, the pipeline checks whether a vehicle was identified at all. If YOLO sees a vehicle and its color and body type match the active FEMA alert profile — a white SUV when the alert says white SUV — a plate-free detection fires. It routes to its own coordinator channel, separate from both standard hits and mismatch alerts.

Current behavior — no readable plate, vehicle profile match

Coordinator receives: 👁️ VEHICLE PROFILE MATCH — NO PLATE READ. Confidence is capped at 75% — it can never score as high as a confirmed plate read. Notification includes YOLO-detected color, body type, a frame thumbnail, and the explicit instruction: Action required: reposition for plate read. Ten-minute cooldown per drone to avoid flooding the channel from a single vehicle.

What plate-free detection is not

It is not a positive identification. It is a repositioning cue. The coordinator's job when they receive one is to direct the volunteer toward a better angle — not to act on the alert as if the plate had been confirmed. The channel separation and the 75% cap exist precisely to preserve that distinction.

What neither path solves: If a suspect switches the plate AND changes the vehicle's visible characteristics — repainting, adding body panels — neither pipeline will find them. That's outside what any automated system can address. Human witnesses remain essential.

Why Honesty Matters Here

A coordinator who trusts our system needs to know exactly what it can and can't do. Each of the three detection channels — confirmed plate hit, plate match with profile mismatch, profile match without plate — carries a different evidentiary weight. We've built the system to make that weight visible, not to flatten everything into a single confidence number that obscures what the underlying signal actually is.

The plate is still the primary key. Everything else is a cue to go find the plate.
